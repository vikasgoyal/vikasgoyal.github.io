The folded year

Naperville, Illinois has twelve months of weather but only seven kinds of it. Fold the calendar in half at January and July and the months land in pairs: December on February, November on March, October on April. Unfold it to see the year as it is actually lived.

Each pair is two months that share almost the same weather — the warm sliver between them is how far autumn runs ahead of spring. Hover a month for its numbers.

Why the two halves don't quite meet

The fold never closes cleanly. Every autumn month sits a little above the spring month it lands on — October above April, September above May — and it happens in the same direction every single time.

That is thermal lag. Ground and water spend the summer absorbing heat and the autumn giving it back, so the year warms up faster than it cools down. The widest gap falls in the May–September pair, where autumn runs a full six degrees ahead.
