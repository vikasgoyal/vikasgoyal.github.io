Not an endpoint.

Somewhere along the way, “don't just say hello” became workplace advice. The intention makes sense: fewer interruptions, more context, less waiting.

But a good intention can feel like a closed door. Especially when you're new, unsure how to ask, or simply used to starting a conversation with a greeting.

We can value someone's time without making warmth unwelcome. Say hello. Add your question when you can. And leave room for the other person to reply when they're ready.

This isn't a rule against “nohello.” It's an invitation to be a little more human with each other.

Open doesn't mean always on.

A hello is an invitation, not a demand for an immediate reply. Focus time, quiet hours, and boundaries still matter.

Context is kind. So is a greeting.

“Hi! Could you help me with this?” is a wonderful start. And if someone starts with just “hi,” that's okay too.
